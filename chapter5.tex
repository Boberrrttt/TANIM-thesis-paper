\chapter{CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS}
{\baselineskip=2\baselineskip

This chapter provides the summary of the results obtained in this study and gives some recommendations for further investigation.

\section{Summary of Findings}

This study developed and evaluated an IoT-based crop recommender system integrated with machine learning for agricultural applications in Bukidnon. The findings demonstrate that the system successfully integrated hardware and software components, including soil sensors, a machine learning model, and web and mobile applications, to provide a functional decision-support tool for farmers. The system was able to measure key soil parameters such as pH, nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), salinity, moisture, and temperature, then transmit these data to a centralized platform for processing and visualization.

The raw dataset used for model training exhibited a significant class imbalance, with Corn accounting for 64.3 percent of the total 3,830 soil records, while minority crop classes such as sweet pepper, ampalaya, and tobacco each contributed fewer than 75 samples. To resolve this disparity, K-Means SMOTE was applied to expand and balance the dataset to 24,620 records with equal representation across all ten crop classes. While this augmentation strategy successfully improved the aggregate performance of the model, it introduces a consideration regarding potential overestimation of performance on underrepresented crop classes, which should be addressed through continued collection of field-validated soil samples in future iterations.

Aggregate evaluation metrics further revealed significant disparities in classification performance across individual crop categories. High-performing classes such as Tobacco and Sorghum achieved F1-scores of approximately 0.965 and 0.925, respectively, attributed to their agronomically distinct soil profiles that allow the model to establish clear decision boundaries. In contrast, Eggplant remained the most challenging class with an F1-score of approximately 0.664, largely due to its overlapping soil nutrient and pH requirements with closely related crops such as Tomato and Sweet Pepper. These findings indicate that while overall model accuracy is satisfactory, per-class performance should be considered when interpreting recommendation outputs for crops with similar agronomic profiles.

Qualitative data gathered through an interview with a Senior Agriculturist from the Department of Agriculture Region 10 highlighted the persistent gap between laboratory-grade soil analysis and field-level practice in Bukidnon. The interview confirmed that the majority of farmers in the region rely on observational methods or Soil Test Kits (STKs) due to the cost and logistical barriers associated with laboratory testing. This finding validates the development of an IoT-based sensor solution capable of providing immediate, field-level categorical soil health insights as a more accessible alternative for smallholder farmers.

Discrepancies observed in sensor readings, particularly in phosphorus levels, were attributed to soil heterogeneity in the sampled areas. Spatial variability in soil composition was identified as a primary driver of measurement fluctuation, as identical sensor units produced varying readings across spatially proximate sampling points. This finding underscores the need for multi-point sampling strategies and spatially aware calibration procedures in future deployments.

The calibration results of the soil pH sensor revealed a non-linear response across different buffer solutions, indicating that a single constant offset correction is insufficient for achieving accurate readings. Validation results further showed that while the sensor outputs were generally aligned with qualitative classifications from STK results, there were significant numerical discrepancies when compared to laboratory measurements from the Department of Agriculture. Error analysis confirmed the presence of noticeable offsets, particularly in potassium and phosphorus values, with Mean Absolute Errors of 108.00 and 36.33, respectively, suggesting limitations in achieving laboratory-grade precision.  Despite this, the system demonstrated reliability in categorical classification (Low, Medium, High), which is essential for practical crop and fertilizer recommendation in field settings.

The machine learning model achieved a global accuracy of 82.29 percent and a balanced accuracy of 82.30 percent, with consistent performance across evaluation metrics, indicating its capability to generate reliable crop recommendations based on soil health data. The fertilizer recommendation engine, grounded in Bureau of Soils and Water Management (BSWM) protocols, successfully generated agronomically sound fertilizer prescriptions validated against published standards. The developed mobile application enabled farmers to monitor soil conditions, view crop predictions, and access fertilizer schedules, while the web application provided administrative functionalities such as farm zoning, farmer data management, and soil health visualization. The system also achieved a System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 84.25, which corresponds to an Excellent rating, indicating that users found the system intuitive and effective for agricultural use.

Overall, the results confirm that all the objectives of the study were successfully achieved. The implementation of the IoT system, along with the development of both mobile and web applications and the integration of a machine learning model and a rule-based fertilizer recommendation engine, demonstrates the feasibility of the proposed system as a comprehensive agricultural decision-support platform for Bukidnon.

\section{Conclusion}

Based on the findings of the study, it can be concluded that the TANIM system successfully implemented its primary objective of developing an IoT-based recommender system integrated with machine learning to monitor soil health parameters and generate data-driven agricultural recommendations for farmers in Bukidnon.

The machine learning model was successfully developed using the LightGBM gradient boosting algorithm, trained on historical soil health data from the Department of Agriculture Region 10 and augmented through K-Means SMOTE to address class imbalance. The optimized pipeline achieved a global accuracy of 82.29\% and a balanced accuracy of 82.30\% across ten crop categories, demonstrating reliable and equitable performance in crop suitability prediction. The rule-based fertilizer recommendation engine, validated against BSWM standards, successfully generated structured fertilizer prescriptions from soil nutrient classifications, confirming its correctness and agricultural soundness across all tested soil profiles.

The mobile application was successfully deployed for farmer use, providing an accessible interface that displays real-time soil health parameters, machine learning-based crop predictions, fertilizer schedules, and phased application timelines. The web application was likewise successfully deployed for administrative users from the Department of Agriculture, enabling centralized monitoring of soil health insights, geographic farm zoning through an integrated GIS mapping feature, and management of farmer profiles and farming session records. Both platforms demonstrated stable performance and cross-device compatibility throughout testing.

The overall crop recommender system achieved a global accuracy of 82.29 percent, and through usability testing involving seven farmers and three Department of Agriculture officials, the system attained a SUS score of 84.25, confirming its practicality and suitability for deployment in Bukidnon's agricultural context. All six system modules passed User Acceptance Testing with a 100 percent pass rate, further establishing the functional reliability of the platform.

However, when sensor readings were compared against Department of Agriculture laboratory results, significant numerical offsets were observed, particularly in phosphorus and potassium measurements. These discrepancies indicate that the current sensor configuration cannot fully replace controlled soil laboratory testing for applications requiring high numerical precision. Nevertheless, the system remains effective as a field-level decision-support tool, providing reliable categorical soil health interpretations that are sufficient for practical crop and fertilizer recommendation purposes.

In comparison to related studies in the field, the TANIM system addresses several existing gaps by providing an end-to-end integrated solution that combines real-time IoT sensing, machine learning-based recommendation, rule-based fertilizer advisory, and dual-platform accessibility through both web and mobile applications. Many studies in this domain focus exclusively on simulation models, isolated sensor systems, or standalone software applications. This study contributes a more comprehensive and deployable solution that is directly grounded in regional soil data and validated through field testing. Overall, the TANIM system demonstrates strong potential in advancing precision agriculture by enabling accessible, real-time, and data-driven decision-making for farmers, especially in regions with limited access to laboratory facilities.
\section{Recommendations}

Based on the conclusions of the study, several recommendations are proposed to further enhance the system and guide future research directions. Improvements in sensor accuracy and hardware reliability are strongly recommended for future iterations of the system. The integration of industrial-grade, high-precision sensors is advised to replace the current low-cost sensor unit, particularly for the measurement of phosphorus and potassium, where the largest deviations from laboratory reference values were observed. Alongside this, the utilization of high-grade calibration tools and certified buffer solutions for multi-point, non-linear calibration procedures is recommended to address the non-linear sensor response behavior identified during evaluation. Polynomial or regression-based calibration models should be implemented and supplemented with periodic field recalibration schedules to minimize systematic measurement errors over time.

The deployment of industrial-grade LoRa modules is further recommended to improve the reliability and range of wireless data transmission, particularly for farm sites located in geographically challenging terrain. Additionally, the standardization of weather-resistant enclosures for all hardware units is advised to ensure the durability and longevity of the sensing system under varying field conditions, including rain, humidity, and direct sunlight exposure common to agricultural environments in Bukidnon.


For the mobile application, it is recommended to introduce greater customizability to accommodate the diverse needs of farmers across different crop types and farm configurations. This may include adjustable recommendation thresholds, personalized farm activity logs, and notification 
settings tailored to individual farming schedules. The addition of an offline functionality mode is also advised, as connectivity limitations in rural agricultural areas remain a practical concern identified during expert consultation. For the web application, enhanced data visualization features are recommended for agricultural administrators, including advanced trend analysis tools, comparative soil health reports across farm zones, and exportable data summaries to support evidence-based policy and resource allocation decisions at the regional level.


Further enhancements to the machine learning pipeline are recommended to improve the accuracy and robustness of crop and fertilizer recommendations. The exploration of Conditional Tabular Generative Adversarial Networks (CTGAN) and other deep generative models is advised as an alternative to K-Means SMOTE for producing balanced, realistic synthetic soil data that more faithfully represents the natural distribution of minority crop classes. This approach may help reduce potential overestimation of model performance on rare crop categories and improve the model's predictive reliability across unseen soil profiles.

It is further recommended to expand the feature set of the model by incorporating environmental and spatial context variables such as weather patterns, rainfall data, terrain elevation, and agro-ecological zone classifications. The inclusion of these factors would address the current limitation of relying exclusively on soil parameters, enabling the system to generate recommendations that are sensitive to seasonal dynamics and climatic variability. Continuous retraining of the model using newly collected field data, including calibration-adjusted sensor readings and laboratory-validated samples, is also recommended to maintain predictive accuracy and adaptability over time. Finally, future research may explore 
the long-term impact of the TANIM system on crop yield outcomes, fertilizer use efficiency, and overall farm sustainability to fully establish its effectiveness as a precision agriculture tool in the Philippine context.
