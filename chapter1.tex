	\section{Background of the Study}
	
	Agriculture is important in the Philippines since it serves as the foundation of food security, livelihood, and rural development. Traditional soil management practices in the Philippines, such as those endorsed by the Department of Agriculture (DA), rely on generalized soil health monitoring and fertilizer recommendations using manual tools like the Minus-One Element Technique (MOET) and the Rapid Soil Test Device (RST). These approaches often fail to address field-specific variability, crop diversity, and seasonal dynamics, leading to suboptimal resource utilization, as reported by the Department of Agriculture Region 10 (2023). Historically, soil testing requires farmers to transport soil samples to nearby laboratories located at Department of Agriculture offices, a process that is often expensive, time-consuming, and inaccessible in remote areas, as highlighted by Pagaduan, J. L. (2025).
	
	In relation to this, the Department of Agriculture – Regional Field Office 10 (DA–RFO 10) conducted a soil sampling drive for rice and corn production areas in the municipalities of Sugbongcogon, Binuangan, Magsaysay, and Gingoog City from March 26–28, 2025. Aggie 10 aimed to complete soil sampling and testing in priority sites across Region 10 by May 2025 to align with the wet cropping calendar. Despite these initiatives, farmers still largely depend on traditional and observational methods. The study by Samaniego, A. L. and Gallego, M. (2024) reported that only 1.03\% of farmers use soil-test kits and another 1.03\% utilize government laboratory services, while the remaining 97.94\% rely on ocular observation or “eyeballing” of soil and crop conditions. Furthermore, crop selection decisions are often based on historical practices rather than data-driven insights, limiting farmers’ ability to adapt to changing soil and climate conditions (Sarma et al., 2024). With advancements in technology such as the Internet of Things (IoT), there is significant potential to improve crop yields and reduce agricultural losses.
	
	Soil health is a fundamental pillar of sustainable agriculture, underpinning crop productivity, food security, and environmental resilience. According to Lal, R. (2024), degraded soils characterized by nutrient deficiencies, imbalanced pH levels, or reduced organic matter lead to lower yields, increased susceptibility to pests, and economic losses for farmers. In Region 10 (Northern Mindanao, Philippines), a major agricultural hub producing rice, corn, banana, and cacao, maintaining optimal levels of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), and a soil pH range of 6.0–7.5 is essential for nutrient availability and healthy crop growth, as discussed by Morgan and Connolly (2013) and Sondhiya and Singh (2024). Data from the Department of Agriculture Region 10 (2024) indicate that acidic soils in Bukidnon restrict phosphorus uptake, while nitrogen deficiencies in rice fields can reduce yields by up to 20\%. Moreover, proper crop selection based on soil condition and season, combined with precise fertilizer application, is crucial to maximizing productivity and minimizing environmental impacts such as nutrient runoff (Patil et al., 2023).
	
	Globally, agricultural losses caused by soil degradation, inefficient nutrient management, and climate variability pose a serious threat to food security and economic stability. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO, 2023) estimates that approximately 33\% of the world’s soils are moderately to highly degraded, resulting in annual crop yield losses of up to 25\% in some regions. The Economics of Land Degradation (ELD) initiative (2015) further reports that land degradation leads to global economic losses of up to USD 10.6 trillion per year, equivalent to nearly 17\% of global Gross Domestic Product (GDP), due to reduced agricultural productivity, ecosystem service loss, and increased vulnerability to climate extremes. These losses are worsened by improper fertilizer use, where over-application leads to nutrient runoff and environmental damage, while under-application results in nutrient deficiencies and yield reduction.
	
	In the Philippine context, these global challenges are strongly reflected at the national and regional levels. The Department of Agriculture (2024) reported that approximately 80\% of agricultural soils in the Philippines are deteriorated, significantly limiting crop productivity and fertilizer efficiency. Climate-related disturbances such as typhoons, droughts, and flooding further contribute to agricultural losses, with the Department of Agriculture estimating ₱785 million in crop damage in late 2024 alone due to successive weather events. In major agricultural regions such as Northern Mindanao (Region 10), nutrient-depleted and acidic soils, combined with non-site-specific fertilizer practices, contribute to yield reductions in staple crops such as rice and corn, increasing farmers’ production costs and reducing income stability. These persistent losses underscore the urgent need for data-driven, location-specific, and climate-aware agricultural decision-support systems.
	
	Advancements in Internet of Things (IoT) technologies and machine learning (ML) offer promising solutions for precision agriculture. IoT-based sensors, including NPK, pH, moisture, and temperature probes, enable continuous soil health monitoring, while machine learning algorithms analyze large and complex datasets to generate data-driven insights and recommendations which was supported by the studies of \cite{Kumar2024} and \cite{Lavanya2024}. Modern farming systems leverage these technologies to optimize crop production, improve fertilizer efficiency, and reduce waste, ultimately enhancing yield and sustainability which was stated in the study of \cite{Elbasi2023}. By integrating Internet of Things (IoT) sensor data with historical records, and agricultural zoning data, intelligent systems can provide crop recommendations tailored to the agricultural conditions of Region 10.
	
	This study proposed a Soil Health Monitoring and Crop Recommendation System that leveraged IoT sensors and machine learning techniques to provide recommendations for crop planting and fertilizer application in Bukidnon. Targeting key crops such as ampalaya, beans (Soybean), cassava, corn, eggplant, sorghum, squash, sweet pepper, tobacco, and tomato, the system integrated soil health parameters (NPK, pH, moisture, salinity, and temperature), soil and crop data from the Department of Agriculture - Region 10, and additional online agricultural resources to predict suitable crops and recommend fertilizer application. By addressing field-specific variability and seasonal dynamics, the proposed system aimed to enhance agricultural productivity, reduce resource waste, and promote sustainable farming practices. Aligned with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 2 (Zero Hunger), 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), and 15 (Life on Land), this research contributed to food security, environmental conservation, and sustainable agricultural development in the Philippines.
	
	
	\section{Statement of the Problem}
	
	
	Traditional soil management and crop selection practices in Bukidnon remain largely generalized and manual, limiting their effectiveness in addressing field-specific agricultural conditions. Farmers primarily rely on standard guidelines and conventional assessment tools provided by the Department of Agriculture, which do not sufficiently consider variations in soil properties, crop requirements, and seasonal environmental changes. This lack of precision often results in inefficient fertilizer application, suboptimal crop selection, reduced yields, increased production costs, and gradual soil degradation (Lal, R., 2023). These issues are particularly evident in Bukidnon, where diverse crops such as Ampalaya, Beans (Soybean), Cassava, Corn, Eggplant, Sorghum, Squash, Sweet Pepper, Tobacco, and Tomato are cultivated under varying soil  conditions.
	
	Despite the importance of soil health information in agricultural decision-making, several problems persist that hinder the effective use of technology and data-driven approaches in the region. These problems include:
	
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Inconsistent and limited availability of accurate, real-time soil health data (pH, NPK, salinity, moisture, and temperature) across different farming zones in Bukidnon, making it difficult to assess actual field conditions.
		\item The absence of an integrated decision-support mechanism that analyzes soil health data, resulting in unreliable or generalized crop suitability and fertilizer recommendation application.
		\item Limited access to user-friendly digital platforms that can translate complex agricultural data into clear, practical recommendations understandable to farmers in Bukidnon.
		\item Uncertainty regarding the effectiveness and scalability of existing IoT-based agricultural monitoring systems in supporting sustainable farming practices across multiple farms and locations in Bukidnon.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	\section{Objectives of the Study}
	
	The main objective of this study is to develop an IoT-based crop recommender system integrated with machine learning for crop recommendation based on soil health parameters (pH, NPK, salinity, moisture, and temperature) for farmers, specifically the following:
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Implement a machine learning model to predict suitable crops and recommend fertilizers application for farming practices in Bukidnon based on soil health data.
		\item Develop a mobile application to monitor soil health, crop predictions, and fertilizer schedules for the farmers.
		\item Develop a website application to monitor soil health insights for different zoning of farms, farmers data and information on their farming timeline.
		\item Test and evaluate the model's efficiency (accuracy, precision, recall \& F1 - Score) and the IoT crop recommendation system's functionality and usability in agricultural settings in Bukidnon.
	\end{enumerate}

	\section{Scope and Limitations}
	
	This study focuses on agricultural areas in Bukidnon, Philippines, where commonly cultivated crops include ampalaya, soybean (beans), cassava, corn, eggplant, sorghum, squash, sweet pepper, tobacco, and tomato. These crops serve as the primary basis for the system's crop recommendation and fertilizer suggestion processes. The system is designed to analyze key soil health parameters acquired from the soil sensor, namely nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), soil moisture, temperature, pH level, and soil salinity. These parameters are integrated with soil and crop data obtained from the Department of Agriculture Region 10 and fertilizer application standards from the Bureau of Soils and Water Management (BSWM), forming the foundation for generating accurate crop recommendations and fertilizer management strategies. Based on the analyzed soil nutrient levels, the system classifies nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium into qualitative grades, such as Low, Medium, or High, and generates corresponding fertilizer prescriptions expressed in kilograms per hectare and converted into standard 50 kg commercial bag quantities. Two fertilizer application options are provided for each recommendation which allows farmers to select the most accessible option based on local supply availability. Furthermore, the system employs an end-to-end data pipeline, from data acquisition to recommendation, wherein sensor-derived soil data is transmitted to a mobile application for processing, crop prediction, and fertilizer output generation, including a phased application schedule covering basal and topdress stages.
	
	The study is geographically limited to selected farms in Bukidnon to ensure relevance to local soil characteristics and agricultural practices. It emphasizes the integration of Internet of Things (IoT)-based soil sensing technology with machine learning techniques for crop recommendation, and rule-based logic grounded in BSWM protocols for fertilizer suggestion. However, the system is limited to the measurement of seven soil parameters and does not account for micronutrients or soil organic matter beyond what is estimated through sensor-derived readings. Additionally, it does not support real-time continuous monitoring, relying instead on periodic data collection and analysis. The fertilizer suggestions generated by the system are further constrained to macronutrients (N, P, K) and do not extend to secondary nutrients or micronutrient supplementation. The implementation is also constrained to a one-to-one configuration, where one hardware unit is paired with a single mobile application. While these limitations may affect the level of detail in the recommendations, they remain sufficient to demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed system within the defined scope.
	
	\section{Conceptual Framework}
	
	In this section, the study’s conceptual framework is presented through an Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, which outlines the structure of the soil health monitoring, crop and fertilizer recommendation system. The framework integrates multiple components, each playing a vital role in developing an efficient, data-driven solution for sustainable agriculture.
	
	\begin{figure}[H]
		\centering
		\caption{IPO Model}
		\label{fig:ipo}
		\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/IPO.pdf}
	\end{figure}
	
	The TANIM system operates upon an Input Process Output model explicitly engineered to generate localized crop and fertilizer recommendations for farmers across Region 10, Philippines. The Input phase captures active soil health telemetry, such as Organic Matter serving as a Nitrogen proxy, Phosphorus, Potassium, soil pH, moisture, salinity, and temperature, gathered continuously via an ESP32 microcontroller and its attached Internet of Things sensor network. To establish a comprehensive agricultural baseline, these localized sensor inputs run parallel to regional weather and climate data, Department of Agriculture Region 10 soil zoning maps, and historical empirical soil records.
	
	Transitioning into the Process layer, these raw inputs undergo a rigorous and multistage computational pipeline. The acquired telemetry is first subjected to programmatic data preprocessing and cleaning protocols, systematically removing empty reads and sensor anomalies to guarantee absolute diagnostic consistency. Within the data integration and contextualization phase, severe historical imbalances present in the agricultural dataset are actively neutralized via K Means synthetic data augmentation, ensuring all crop classes hold equal representational weight. Furthermore, a specialized feature engineering protocol derives crucial agricultural interaction variables, such as pH controlled nutrient availability, dynamically from the raw data. This deeply contextualized and balanced matrix is subsequently evaluated by an Optuna optimized Light Gradient Boosting Machine framework, which analyzes complex soil condition patterns to precisely isolate and predict biological crop suitability.
	
	Governed by this classification analysis, the Output phase is generated. The system delivers actionable soil health insights alongside high confidence and zone specific primary and alternative crop recommendations. These predictive classifications bridge seamlessly into secondary subsystems that formulate appropriate and crop specific fertilizer application strategies. The operational validity of the entire pipeline is continuously tracked via rigorous performance metrics, including analytical confusion matrices, prediction error monitoring, and F1 score dashboards, which are compiled natively into visual reports and delivered through an accessible user interface. Ultimately, this seamless and data driven cycle equips agricultural operators with the empirical insight required to optimize resource utilization, maximize productivity, and enforce sustainable farming practices.
	
	\section{Theoretical Framework}
	
	This study utilized Precision Agriculture Theory, a strategy that gathers, processes data driven insights to optimize farming practices based on conditions rather than relying on generalized farming practices recommendations, which is stated by the Father of precision agriculture, Dr. Pierre C. Robert in his study in  \cite{RobertTribute} which states that an integrated farming approach designed to enhance long-term production efficiency, productivity, and profitability while minimizing environmental impacts. He stated that it involves using technology and data to optimize farming practices at a site-specific level. The theory highlights the importance of applying the right input, at the right place, at the right time, and in the right amount to maximize productivity while conserving resources.
	
	With the integration of IoT sensors which will provide measurements of soil health parameters the precision agriculture theory will be fully operational. The soil parameters will serve as a foundation to generate actionable recommendations for farmers. Moreover, machine learning boosting algorithms will be used to analyze soil and environmental patterns to predict crop suitability and yield potential.
	
		
	\begin{figure}[H]
		\centering
		\caption{Precision Agriculture Cycle}
		\label{fig:precisionagriculture}
		\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{figures/PrecisionAgriculture.pdf}
	\end{figure}
	
	Figure \ref{fig:precisionagriculture} explains the Precision Agriculture Cycle on how farming decisions become data-driven. It starts with acquiring data through IoT sensors and other tools that measure soil and environmental conditions. This data is then processed into insights to understand soil fertility and crop needs. Using these insights, decisions are made with the help of Machine Learning (ML) for prediction. The next step is taking action, where farmers apply precise fertilizers, choose suitable crops, or adjust irrigation. Finally, the results and learnings feed back into the system, ensuring continuous improvement in productivity, efficiency, and sustainability.
	
	By combining Machine Learning’s predictive capability, the study applies the principles of precision agriculture in a holistic manner. This ensures that recommendations for crop selection and fertilizer application are data-driven, addressing the variability of soils in Bukidnon. Thus, Precision Agriculture Theory serves as the guiding lens that connects IoT-based data collection and machine learning prediction to support decision-making, ultimately promoting sustainable and efficient farming practices.
	
	\section{Definition of Terms}
	
	This study defined the following terms that are mentioned in this study.
	\begin{description}
		\item[Agriculture.] 
		The science, art, and practice of cultivating plants and livestock, serving as the foundation for food security, livelihoods, and rural development in regions like the Philippines.
		\item[Agricultural Zoning.] 
		The practice of designating specific areas for agricultural use to conserve farmland, prevent urban conflicts, and promote orderly rural growth, often including minimum lot sizes and restrictions on non-farm activities.
		\item[Boosting Algorithms.] 
		A family of machine learning techniques that convert weak learners into strong ones by iteratively training models to correct errors from previous ones, commonly used in regression and classification tasks.
		\item[Crops.] 
		Plants or plant products grown and harvested extensively for profit or subsistence, categorized into food, feed, fiber, oil, ornamental, and industrial types based on their uses.
		\item[Data Augmentation.]
		A process of artificially increasing the size and diversity of a training dataset by generating new synthetic samples for the existing data.
		\item[Exponential Moving Average (EMA).]
		A smoothing technique that reduces noise in sequential data by giving more weight to recent values. It uses a smoothing factor $\alpha$ to control how quickly older data loses influence, producing more stable sensor readings for analysis.
		\item[Feature Engineering.] 
		The process of using domain knowledge to create, transform, or select input variables (features) from raw data to improve the performance of machine learning models.
		\item[Fertilizer.] 
		A natural or synthetic substance added to soil to supply essential nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, enhancing plant growth and productivity in agriculture.
		\item[Internet of Things (IoT) in Agriculture.] 
		The integration of connected sensors, devices, and networks in farming to monitor soil, weather, crops, and livestock, enabling data-driven decisions for improved efficiency and sustainability.
		\item[K-means clustering.] 
		An unsupervised machine learning algorithm that groups data into K clusters by assigning each data point to the nearest cluster center (centroid). It repeatedly updates the centroids and reassigns points until the clusters become stable.
		\item[Machine Learning.] 
		A subset of artificial intelligence that enables computers to learn from data and improve performance on tasks without explicit programming, often using algorithms to identify patterns and make predictions.
		\item[NPK.]
		The abbreviation for the three primary macronutrients in soil and fertilizers: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K), essential for plant growth, development, and overall health.
		\item[Optuna.] 
		An open-source hyperparameter optimization framework that automatically searches for the best hyperparameter values for a machine learning model using a process called automated hyperparameter tuning. 
		\item[Precision Agriculture.]
		A farming management strategy that uses data from sensors, GPS, and analytics to optimize inputs like water and fertilizers, responding to variability in crops for improved efficiency and sustainability.
		\item[Soil Health.]
		The continued capacity of soil to function as a vital living ecosystem that sustains plants, animals, and humans, encompassing biological, physical, and chemical properties.
		\item[Soil pH.]
		A measure of soil acidity or alkalinity on a scale from 0 to 14, indicating hydrogen ion concentration, which influences nutrient availability and plant growth.
		\item[Soil Salinity.]
		The accumulation of soluble salts in soil that can impair plant growth, often measured by electrical conductivity, with levels above 4 dS/m considered saline.
		\item[Soil Test Kits (STK).] 
		Portable tools developed by the Department of Agriculture Philippines used to measure basic soil parameters such as nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and pH, providing quick on-site soil assessment for crop and fertilizer management.
		\item[Sustainable Agriculture.]
		Farming practices that meet current food needs while preserving resources for future generations, balancing environmental health, economic viability, and social equity.	
	\end{description}
